Layer 2 — Methodology

Aurora Workflow Orchestration (AWO)

AWO defines how compliant research under the Aurora ecosystem must be structured: workflow phases, artifact requirements, role separation, and reproducibility guarantees.

What is AWO?

Aurora Workflow Orchestration converts governance rules (ARI) and disclosure obligations (NTS) into a concrete, repeatable research methodology. It ensures that scientific work remains reconstructible, falsifiable, and independently auditable even years later.

Core Responsibilities

  • Define workflow phases and valid transitions
  • Specify required artifacts and minimum content requirements
  • Establish role separation boundaries (no self-attestation)
  • Integrate Neurotransparency (NTS) at required workflow points
  • Provide deterministic schema inputs to CRI-CORE enforcement

Position in the Aurora Stack

AWO sits between governance (ARI) and enforcement (CRI-CORE), ensuring research is structured before enforcement occurs.

  • Layer 1 — ARI: Governance & metadata law
  • Layer 0 — NTD/NTS: Cognitive provenance requirements
  • Layer 3 — CRI-CORE: Enforcement of workflow + artifacts
  • Layer 4 — Case Studies: Real-world implementation of AWO
