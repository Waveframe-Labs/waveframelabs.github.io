Layer 2 — Methodology

Aurora Workflow Orchestration (AWO)

AWO defines how research under the Aurora ecosystem is conducted in practice. It translates institutional rules into executable, auditable workflows.

What is AWO?

Aurora Workflow Orchestration is the methodological layer that operationalizes governance requirements into concrete research procedures. It defines roles, artifacts, checkpoints, and validation stages for AI–human collaboration.

AWO answers the question: “How must compliant research actually be run?”

Core Responsibilities

  • Define structured research workflows and role separation.
  • Specify required artifacts (manifests, logs, attestations).
  • Enforce audit-first design and reproducibility constraints.
  • Integrate Neurotransparency requirements into daily research practice.
  • Provide deterministic inputs to enforcement tooling (CRI-CORE).

Relationship to Other Layers

  • Layer 1 — ARI: Supplies binding governance rules.
  • Layer 0 — Neurotransparency: Defines epistemic constraints.
  • Layer 3 — CRI-CORE: Enforces AWO workflows mechanically.
  • Layer 4 — Case Studies: Demonstrate AWO in applied research.
