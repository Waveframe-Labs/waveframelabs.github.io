Layer 0 — Epistemic Foundation

Neurotransparency Doctrine

The Neurotransparency Doctrine establishes the epistemic necessity for traceable, attributable, and reconstructible cognition in AI–human research.

What is the Neurotransparency Doctrine?

The Neurotransparency Doctrine defines why modern research requires explicit visibility into how conclusions are produced. As AI systems increasingly participate in reasoning, the provenance of cognition itself becomes a scientific variable.

The doctrine asserts that results cannot be meaningfully evaluated without understanding the cognitive processes — human and artificial — that generated them.

Core Epistemic Principles

  • Traceability: Every reasoning step must be attributable to a source.
  • Attribution: Human and AI contributions must be distinguishable.
  • Reconstructibility: Claims must be reproducible from recorded inputs.
  • Epistemic continuity: Breaks in provenance invalidate downstream claims.
  • Accountability: Responsibility follows cognitive influence.

Relationship to Other Aurora Layers

  • Layer 1 — ARI: Converts doctrine into binding institutional law.
  • Layer 2 — AWO: Implements doctrine as operational workflows.
  • Layer 3 — CRI-CORE: Enforces doctrine mechanically.
  • Layer 4 — Case Studies: Demonstrate doctrine-aligned research.

Doctrine vs. Specification

The Neurotransparency Doctrine defines epistemic necessity. The Neurotransparency Specification defines enforceable requirements.

In short: the doctrine answers why transparency is required; the specification answers how it is enforced.
