Layer 0A — Epistemic Foundation

Why Neurotransparency?

The Neurotransparency Doctrine is the foundational statement of Waveframe Labs. It argues that in AI–assisted research, it is no longer sufficient to ask, “Is this result correct?” We must also ask, “How did this result come into existence, and whose cognition shaped it?”

Neurotransparency demands that the cognitive process behind research — human and machine — be traceable, attributable, and reconstructible. Without this, reproducibility becomes a matter of trust in a black box rather than a property that can be audited.

Core Principles

1. Cognition is an artefact

Human reasoning, AI model output, and the orchestration between them are treated as first-class artefacts. They must be logged and versioned, not left implicit.

2. Attribution over mystique

Claims must be attributable to specific workflows, prompts, models, and human decisions. “The AI did it” is not an acceptable epistemic explanation.

3. Reconstructibility over performance

A result that cannot be reconstructed is epistemically weaker than a slightly “worse” result whose provenance is fully known.

4. Governance as a first-class object

Governance is not paperwork after the fact. It is embedded into workflows, tools, and repositories from the start.

Relation to the Specification (NTS)

The doctrine is philosophical and justificatory. It explains why neurotransparency is necessary. The Neurotransparency Specification (NTS) is the normative standard that follows from it: concrete requirements for logs, metadata, role separation, and attestations.

Downstream Impact

Every other layer in the Aurora Hierarchy is accountable to the doctrine:

  • ARI must govern in a way that preserves neurotransparency.
  • AWO must encode neurotransparency in its workflows.
  • CRI-CORE must enforce neurotransparency mechanically.
  • Case studies must demonstrate neurotransparency in practice.
