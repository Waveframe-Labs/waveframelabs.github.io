The Aurora Hierarchy

The Aurora Ecosystem is organized into strictly layered subsystems. Each layer has a distinct authority domain and may not subsume or redefine the layer above it.

Authority flows downward. Enforcement flows upward.

Overview

The hierarchy separates legitimacy (what rules and commitments exist) from implementation (how those commitments are executed). This separation prevents governance drift, epistemic ambiguity, and enforcement collapse.

This page is a canonical overview of the layer model only. Content authority remains with the referenced documents and repositories.

The Layers (L0–L6)

L0 — Governance & Epistemics (Authority Layer)

Defines epistemic legitimacy, provenance requirements, and institutional authority. This layer is normative and constitutional: nothing below it may redefine legitimacy, authority boundaries, or traceability requirements.

Components: ARI, NTD, NTS

L1 — Method (Operational Semantics)

Structures how governed reasoning is performed and recorded. Method operationalizes L0 requirements into workflows and artifact classes, but does not define legitimacy and does not enforce compliance.

Component: AWO

L2 — Language & Ontology (Meaning Layer)

Stabilizes meaning across artifacts using controlled vocabularies, symbols, and semantic compression. This layer affects interpretation, not authority.

Components: Lexon, Signum, Glyphtrace

L3 — Lineage (Memory Layer)

Preserves historical continuity of claims and artifacts by maintaining dependency and provenance chains. This layer records history; it does not judge correctness.

Component: Tracelink

L4 — Validation & Enforcement (Execution Layer)

Mechanically enforces contracts and invariants defined upstream (L0–L1). CRI-CORE validates artifacts against schemas and constraints and prevents non-compliant execution. It cannot define rules; it only enforces those defined above.

Component: CRI-CORE

L5 — Publication & Distribution (Output Layer)

Produces public-facing, frozen artifacts (e.g., PDFs, releases, metadata-injected outputs). Publication does not imply legitimacy; it reflects prior validation and governance constraints.

Components: Forge, Stamp

L6 — Research Outputs (Application Layer)

Applied work produced under the ecosystem. Case studies demonstrate the stack in practice and generate scientific or applied claims. Case studies may not modify upstream layers.

Examples: Waveframe v4.0, Societal Health Simulator

Why the Hierarchy Matters

Without strict layering, research systems collapse into ad hoc practices where governance, method, enforcement, and meaning blur together. The hierarchy keeps legitimacy (L0) distinct from process (L1), semantics (L2), history (L3), enforcement (L4), publication (L5), and applications (L6).

The result is a system where claims can be traced to explicit commitments, artifacts can be validated against explicit contracts, and outputs can be cited without ambiguity.
