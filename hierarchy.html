Overview

The Aurora Hierarchy is the institutional backbone of Waveframe Labs. It separates why we govern AI–human research from how we do it, who has authority, and where those rules are enforced. 

Layer 0A   Neurotransparency Doctrine        (Why)
Layer 0B   Neurotransparency Specification   (What is required)
Layer 1    Aurora Research Initiative (ARI)  (Who governs)
Layer 2    Aurora Workflow Orchestration     (How work is done)
Layer 3    CRI-CORE Enforcement Engine       (Who enforces)
Layer 4    Case Studies                      (Proof in practice)
Layer 5    Tools & Infrastructure            (Operational capability)
Layer 0A — Neurotransparency Doctrine

Establishes the epistemic motivation for Waveframe Labs. It defines cognitive failure modes (opacity, untraceable AI contribution, unverifiable claims) and argues that neurotransparency — traceable, reconstructible cognition — is a prerequisite for trustworthy AI-assisted research.

Primary artefact: Doctrine PDF and repository.
See: Neurotransparency Doctrine →

Layer 0B — Neurotransparency Specification (NTS)

Converts the doctrine into normative requirements. Describes what must be logged, how attribution works, and which properties a “neurotransparent” workflow must satisfy.

Primary artefact: NTS repository and specification PDF.

Layer 1 — Aurora Research Initiative (ARI)

ARI defines institutional authority: who is allowed to approve work, what roles exist, and how governance decisions are documented. It is the governance layer that owns the doctrine and delegates responsibilities to workflows and tooling.

Primary artefact: ARI repository (charter, governance model, metadata policy).

Layer 2 — Aurora Workflow Orchestration (AWO)

AWO is the method layer. It specifies manifests, attestation logs, approval flows, and role separation between “builder” and “reviewer” agents in AI–human collaborations.

Primary artefact: AWO normative specification and supporting docs.

Layer 3 — CRI-CORE Enforcement Engine

CRI-CORE is the enforcement layer that makes AWO non-optional. It validates metadata, directory structure, manifests, and attestation against the doctrine and specification, producing cryptographically verifiable compliance artefacts.

Primary artefact: CRI-CORE runtime and validators (in development).

Layer 4 — Case Studies

Case studies show the hierarchy working end-to-end. Each project demonstrates an AI–human research pipeline running under AWO and enforced (progressively) by CRI-CORE.

Examples: Waveframe v4.0, Societal Health Simulator, Tauwarp (archived).

Layer 5 — Tools & Infrastructure

Operational tools that make governance practical: PDF generation, metadata injection, validation, and release automation.

Examples: Waveframe PDF Forge, metadata pipeline, Stamp, validator suite.
